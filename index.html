FIELD GUIDE / WORKING DEFINITIONS

Three frequencies. One usable window.

LUF
Lowest usable frequency

Below this, absorption and noise make reliable communication difficult. It changes with power, noise, time and path—not just ionization.

FOT
Frequency of optimum transmission

Often approximated as 85% of the path MUF for planning, balancing resilience against higher-band performance.

MUF
Maximum usable frequency

Above this, the assumed ionosphere cannot bend the ray back for the chosen path geometry. An oblique path may work above vertical fo.

SFI / SSN → F-layer density

Higher sustained solar activity often raises electron density and foF2, increasing the frequencies an HF path can support. These indices are correlated, not independent physical forces.

Sunlight / flares → D absorption

Daylight increases D-region collisions and lower-band attenuation. Solar flare X-rays can cause sudden shortwave fadeouts; this lab does not model flare intensity.

Kp / A → disturbed propagation

Geomagnetic disturbances can produce F-region changes, auroral absorption and irregular paths, especially at high latitudes. Effects vary by location and time.

+3 dB ≈ 2× power while +10 dB = 10× power. Changing 100 W to 200 W adds only about 3 dB.

02

Double path distance → ~6 dB extra FSPL in the free-space term. Skywave has additional ionospheric and ground losses.

Favorable SNR ≠ guaranteed contact. MUF, skip-zone geometry, multimode fading and external interference still decide whether a link is practical.
